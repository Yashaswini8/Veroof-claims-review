VeRoof reads the claim form, the repair estimate or FIR, and the customer’s own account of the incident — then reviews them against a motor insurance policy clause by clause. The result is a cited claim review: what’s complete, what’s consistent, which clauses apply, and a clear recommendation.

Accident or theft · two-wheelers & cars · every finding cites its source document and clause.

⛏  How a review works
1

Three documents in

A claim form, a repair estimate or FIR, and the customer’s incident description.

The estimate is used for accidents; the FIR for theft. Every field is read as-is.

2

Structured extraction

Every date, amount, part and flag is pulled out and validated — nothing is invented.

Dates, amounts and declared attachments become flags the checks can reason over.

3

Deterministic checks

Completeness, intimation windows, IDV limits and exclusion triggers are decided by rules, not opinion.

Each check maps to a clause in the policy document, with a pass / fail / escalate verdict.

4

Clause-grounded report

Contradictions are surfaced, citations expand to the source clause, and uncertain cases go to a human.

Approve, request information, escalate or reject — always with cited evidence.
